Earlier this year, we asked the Debezium community to tell us how they're using the project, what's working, and where we need to do better.
We received numerous responses, and we appreciate the candid, detailed, and genuinely useful information that people took the time to share.

This post is going to walk you through all the key findings.
We're sharing everything with the public, which will include not only the things that we're proud of or that surprised us, but equally important the things we need to fix.

+++<!-- more -->+++

== Who Responded

The Debezium community is more diverse than you might expect.
Respondents came from over 30 industries: Software and SaaS, FinTech, Healthcare, Gaming, Logistics, Retail, Manufacturing, Media, Government, Insurance, IoT, Gambling, Fashion, Telecommunications, and even Water and Waste Utility services.
It turns out, Change Data Capture (CDC) is clearly a universal need.

The majority of the respondents, 60.9%, work at organizations with more than 500 employees, which tells us Debezium is firmly established in enterprise environments.
But smaller teams are well represented too, with 10.1% coming from organizations of 11-50 people, and 7.2% from teams of 10 or fewer.

Across these organizations, roles ranged from architects and engineers to directors, VPs, and MIS specialists.
The people operating Debezium in production sit across the entire technical org chart.

== The Numbers We're Most Proud Of

Let's start with the headline: 65.2% of respondents gave Debezium a 5 out of 5 when asked how likely they are to recommend it.
Another 20.3% gave it a 4. Only 1.4% rated it a 2, and no one rated it a 1.

== The Skills Debezium Requires

Operating Debezium well requires a genuine breadth of expertise.
Respondents identified five key skill areas:

Database internals and administration::
Understanding replication mechanisms used by the source database, often times at a low level.

Kafka and data streaming::
Familiarity with Kafka, Kafka Connect, and the broader streaming ecosystem.

Operational and DevOps::
Kubernetes, Docker, Helm, Terraform

Architectural and system design::
Designing end-to-end data flows, managing integrations, and reasoning about distributed systems.

Programming and configuration::
Java is needed if you need to create any custom converters, transforms, or implement custom connectors.
It's also critical if you need to do any sort of memory debugging due to out of memory concerns.

In total, 79.7% of the respondents said their team has the skills to operate Debezium.
That means **1 in 5 respondents don't believe their team has the skills**.
That's a meaningful gap, and it has real implications for what we need to improve on in terms of documentation, tooling, and the overall user experience.

== Where Debezium Falls Short

When we asked what's difficult to use, what's missing, and what the website lacks, the responses clustered into clear themes.

=== Documentation Gaps

The overall assessment of Debezium's documentation was positive, respondents recognized it as thorough.
But "thorough" and "complete" are vastly different things.
What's missing, according to the community:

* **Deep-drive guides for managed services**: there is a clear desire for in-depth guides that outline environment-specific setups for cloud providers.
* **Operational and troubleshooting content**: monitoring, debugging, management commands, and best practices are hard to find or absent.
* **Better Search**: the website search experience needs significant improvement
* **End-to-end examples**: while Debezium does provide a wide array of examples, the community is looking for complex, realistic demos covering multi-connector setups and production-grade configurations.

=== Management and Observability

The single most consistent request was for a **Web UI or console portal** for managing Debezium deployments.
When asked which runtimes they'd manage via a Web UI, respondents said Kafka Connect (75.4%), Debezium Server (40.6%), and Kubernetes/OpenShift using Debezium Platform (34.8%).

=== Missing capabilities

Specific gaps that multiple respondents highlighted include:

* Automatic incremental snapshots for newly added tables, or the ability to do partial snapshots.
* DLQ (Dead Letter Queue) improvement for sink connectors including auto-reprocessing and better handling of missed events.
* Graceful failover support
* Schema history cleanup and compaction
* Better support for combining or querying records from other tables during transformations
* Time-series and ML/vector database integrations, more on this below

== The Contributor Community Has Room To Grow

Our respondents indicated that 23.2% of them have contributed to Debezium in the past, a meaningful base for an open-source project, but also a clear signal that most users haven't made the jump from consumer to contributor.
Of those who have contributed, 75% did not find the experience challenging, which suggests the barrier isn't the contributing process itself necessarily.

For the 25% who did find the contributing experience challenging, the main friction points were environment and setup, code familiarity, and integration tests.
These are the most common concerns when first contributing to a new code base.
If we can find ways to help close those gaps and make it easier for the other 76.8% to find a way in, there's a real opportunity to grow our contributor base.

== How People Find Debezium

The majority of respondents found Debezium through our website (46.4%) or using search engines (17%).
Social media accounted for approximately 16% while technical conferences accounted for 13%.

The dominance of organic search and direct website traffic tells us that Debezium's reputation does the heavy lifting.
People find it because they're looking for a solution and it's surfaces well on searches, and not because of any marketing campaigns.

== What We're Doing About It

The community feedback is extremely valuable to the project team.
Acting on it is what matters, and here's where the survey results are directly influencing our priorities:

**Documentation**::
We are actively discussing how we can improve our overall website, including the documentation.
This includes providing better troubleshooting and operational guides, managed service setup guides, and improved examples.
We've also heard the feedback about better search, and we are actively exploring options.
We are also integrating documentation directly into the Debezium Platform, providing a better in-context experience.

**Management tooling**::
The demand for a web-based user interface is clear and consistent.
The Debezium Platform is our answer to this, and the survey results reinforce that we need to continue investing in it.
Monitoring and observability, along with guided workflows, are being integrated into the Platform. +
 +
Looking further ahead, notifications for incremental snapshots and Kubernetes event dispatching from the Debezium operator are already planned.
We're also exploring CLI-based tooling and host-based deployment options for Debezium Server; nothing to announce just yet, but it's actively being explored, and we hope to have more soon.

**Connector coverage**::
Time-series and vector databases are clearly on the community's mind, and they're on ours too.
Debezium Server source connectors for Milvus and Qdrant are on the roadmap.
A new graph database source connector, a SQLite source connector, and an Elasticsearch sink connector are all planned for later this year.

**Operational improvements**::
Incremental snapshots and observability are both active areas.
Micrometer monitoring via OpenTelemetry is on the roadmap.
Multithreaded single table snapshots were introduced earlier this year to provide scalability for initial and blocking snapshots.
Read-only incremental snapshots for SQL Server and Oracle are planned for a future release.
