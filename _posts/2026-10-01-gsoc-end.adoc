The Google Summer of Code 2026 program has officially concluded for Debezium, and we are delighted to announce that all our contributors successfully passed their final evaluations! 🎉

Over the past few months, our contributors have worked closely with their mentors and the wider community to design, implement, test, and document new capabilities across the Debezium ecosystem.

Their work was not simply an academic exercise or a collection of experimental prototypes. These contributions address real use cases and are already valuable to the many companies and developers who rely on Debezium. The results of their work are included in Debezium 3.7, whose final release will be officially announced soon.

Before looking at what comes next, let’s take a moment to celebrate the contributors and the projects they delivered.

+++<!-- more -->+++

== The GSoc 2026 projects

This year’s projects explored different areas of the Debezium ecosystem, including developer tooling, deployment, artificial intelligence, vector databases, and new data sources. Each project presented its own technical challenges and created new opportunities for the community.

== More than a summer project

Although the GSoC program has formally ended, we do not see this milestone as the end of our contributors’ journey with Debezium.

During the program, they became part of the Debezium community. They participated in technical discussions, collaborated with maintainers, received feedback, and contributed capabilities that will be used in real-world environments.

We warmly encourage all our GSoC contributors to remain involved, continue improving their projects, review issues and pull requests, and bring new ideas to the community. Open source contributions do not have to stop when a formal program ends. GSoC introduced the contributors to the project, but the relationships and collaborations built during these months can continue well beyond it.

== What's next

Each contributor prepared a dedicated blog post describing their project.
The posts will be published during October, and they should cover the original motivation, the technical approach and the final results.

We also invited the students to demonstrate their projects during the Debezium Weekly Forum.

These sessions will give the community an opportunity to:

- See the new capabilities in action
- Learn about the technical decisions behind each project
- Ask questions directly to the contributors
- Provide feedback based on real-world requirements
- Explore possible future improvements and contributions

In November 2026, we will conclude this journey with a final showcase bringing together all the GSoC projects.

More details about the presentations and the final showcase will be shared through the official Debezium channels.

== Thank you!

Running a successful GSoC program requires much more than writing code. It requires contributors willing to learn and accept feedback, mentors who dedicate their time and experience, and a community that welcomes new participants and supports their growth.

We would like to thank all our contributors for their commitment and hard work throughout the program. We also want to thank the mentors and every community member who reviewed proposals, joined discussions or helped the contributors along the way.

Again, congratulations once again to all our GSoC 2026 contributors for successfully passing their final evaluations!
